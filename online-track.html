What is it? [goto top]

A day of programming activities, competitive programming contest (in a team of at most 3 members), meeting with other high school students interesting in CS, etc. The main thing is to have fun. At this point, the programming languages used are C++, Python, and Java. There are three categories: Advanced, Intermediate and Beginner. The Beginner category is a non-competitive category. You can view the Beginner category as a workshop. Teachers are welcome to join their students in the Beginner category. Here's our flyer [TO UPDATE].

Students interested in taking part in the online track of CCCS High School Programming Contest #11 will invite a teacher to be their proctor and register their school as a CCCS Host Site. The teacher can choose to open your site to students from other schools.

Note that you need not form a school team. A team can be made up of students from different schools. But all members of a team must be at the same site. You can choose to be a team of one and work alone.

To register a CCCS Host Site, proctor, student and team, see section Where do I register? below.

The contest committee reserves the right to conduct brief Zoom technical defense interviews ("viva voce") with top-ranking online teams (1st–5th place) to explain their code logic prior to finalizing official standings and awarding tuition scholarships.

This is the first time more than 10 years we are expanding to an online category. There will be hiccups. Please bear with us. We will be asking you for feedback to make future contests better.

Where do I register? [goto top]

To run a CCCS Host Site at a school for the online track, you'll need a proctor, probably a teacher (or an approved adult school sponsor such as a counselor or librarian). The teacher-proctor can choose to open the CCCS Host Site to students from other schools.

Running a CCCS Host Site is as simple as completing the form below and proctoring the programming contest in a classroom. Here's a guideline.

Eligibility and Team Rules

  • Track Eligibility: The online track is open to international high schools and domestic high schools located more than a 2.5-hour drive from Columbia College. Schools within the 2.5-hour radius may apply to participate under a Travel Hardship Exception.
  • Team Composition: Teams may consist of 1 to 3 students. Cross-school teams are permitted.
  • Advance Registration: All participants and teams must register in advance. Teams must compete during their designated contest window.
  • Open Host Sites: During registration, teachers may opt to designate their school as an "Open Host Site" to welcome homeschooled students or external participants from other schools.

Proctoring and Testing Environment

  • Approved Proctors: An approved adult school sponsor must be a teacher, school counselor, or librarian. Proctors cannot be biologically or legally related to any participating student.
  • Proctor-to-Student Ratio: A minimum of 1 proctor per 15 students is required at all times.
  • Testing Location: All team members must be physically co-located in a supervised school environment (such as a computer lab or library) under the direct supervision of their proctor.

Technical Preparation

  • Confirm Devices and Network Access: Determine whether students will use school lab computers or personal laptops. Before contest day, confirm that school Wi-Fi, guest credentials, and firewall permissions are configured and tested for those devices.
  • Test Platform Accessibility: In the actual testing room, verify that your browser is compatible and can load the contest platform (https://hackerrank.com) without network blocks or technical disruptions.

Permitted and Prohibited Materials

  • Reference Books: Each student may bring one commercially published programming language reference guide or beginner textbook focused on syntax, mechanics, and standard libraries (e.g., Python Crash Course, Core Java). Books dedicated primarily to Data Structures, Algorithms, or Competitive Programming (including any book with those terms in the title) are strictly prohibited.
  • Writing Supplies: Students may bring blank scratch paper and pens or pencils.
  • Allowed Websites. Internet access is strictly restricted to the HackerRank contest platform (https://hackerrank.com). Accessing any other websites, including online documentation, search engines, forums, or personal code repositories, is strictly prohibited.
  • Prohibited Software and AI Tools. Generative AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and GitHub Copilot are strictly prohibited. External messaging apps and local IDEs with AI autocomplete features are also banned.
